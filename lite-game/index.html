What is a covered call?

You own shares. You sell someone the right to buy them at a specific price (the strike). They pay you premium for that right upfront.

If shares stay below the strike at expiry: the option expires worthless. You keep the premium and still own the shares.

If shares rise past the strike: you still keep the premium, but your upside is capped at the strike price. That's the tradeoff.

Intermediate · 8 weeks

Premium Income

The setup

You still own 100 shares of DEMO at $100.00. This time the market falls −1% every week.

Before each week starts, you pick a strike and sell a covered call. You collect the premium immediately. If the stock closes below the strike at expiry, the call expires worthless and you keep the full premium.

New concept: intrinsic vs extrinsic

Premium = intrinsic + extrinsic.

  • Intrinsic — how far in-the-money the strike is × 100 shares
  • Extrinsic — time value + volatility. Decays to zero at expiry

You'll see this breakdown for every strike you consider.

03
Advanced · 8 weeks

The Full Picture

The setup

Same 100 shares of DEMO at $100.00. The market is now mixed — some weeks up, some down, some flat. The sequence is seeded so it's the same every time you play.

Each week you decide: sell ITM for max income, ATM for balance, OTM for upside room, or go uncovered and keep full participation.

The tradeoff

When shares rally past your strike, the call goes ITM — you owe the buyer the intrinsic difference. You still keep the premium you collected, but your upside above the strike is gone.

The question isn't "which strike is right?" It's "which tradeoff can I live with this week?"
